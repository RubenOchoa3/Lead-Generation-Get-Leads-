How to use this toolkit

Most cleaning problems aren't about effort — they're about nobody writing down exactly what "clean" means for your building. These pages fix that in about 30 minutes.

  1. Mark up the room-by-room checklist (Part 1). Cross out what doesn't apply, add anything unique to your space, and set how often each item should happen. This becomes your written scope.
  2. Give a copy to whoever cleans your building. A clear list removes the "that wasn't part of the job" argument.
  3. Walk the building once a month with the scorecard (Part 2). Ten minutes, one sheet, a score you can compare month to month.
  4. Hiring or switching? Use Parts 3 and 4 so every company quotes the same scope and you compare real numbers, not sales pitches.
  5. Problems that won't go away? Follow the steps in Part 5 before you start over with someone new.
Tip: Keep a simple log on your phone — date, room, what was missed, a photo. A specific list gets fixed; "it's not clean enough" gets ignored.

Part 3

12 questions to ask before you hire or switch

Ask every company the same questions. Their answers tell you more than the price does.

  1. Will you give me a written scope — every task, by room, with how often it's done?
  2. How many hours per visit and how many people does your price assume?
  3. Is the price fixed monthly? What exactly would make it go up?
  4. Are supplies and consumables included, or billed separately? At what markup?
  5. What costs extra — after-hours, emergency calls, high windows, floor care, carpets? Can I see those rates in writing?
  6. Can you send a current certificate of insurance (general liability and workers' comp)? Are your people bonded?
  7. Who inspects the work, and how often? Will I see the results?
  8. If something is missed, how fast do you fix it — and is that in writing?
  9. Will the same crew clean my building each time? How do you handle keys and alarm codes?
  10. How long is the contract? Is there an auto-renewal? How do I cancel?
  11. Can I talk to a current customer with a building like mine?
  12. Who do I call when there's a problem — and will that person answer?

Red flags

  • A bid far below the others. Lower prices usually mean fewer hours in your building. Ask what's been left out.
  • No written scope — just "general office cleaning."
  • Surprise line items: supply markups, fuel or trip charges, after-hours premiums that weren't in the first quote.
  • Long contracts with auto-renewal and a short cancellation window.
  • No insurance certificate when you ask. These people will be in your building after hours.
  • Nobody inspects the work except you.

Part 5

When your cleaner keeps missing things

  1. Write it down. Two weeks of your log (date, room, what was missed, photo).
  2. Check the scope. Is the missed task actually in the written scope? If not, that's the first fix — add it.
  3. Go above the crew. Send the log to the owner or account manager, not the night crew.
  4. Ask for a written plan with what changes, by when, and a recheck date.
  5. Recheck with the scorecard (Part 2) on that date.
  6. Use your contract. Ask for a credit, or use the termination terms. Then get new bids with Parts 3 and 4.
