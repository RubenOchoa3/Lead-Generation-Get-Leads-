# Building the offer — $100M Offers method, applied to R&S

Use this before writing any cold email. The sources agree the **offer is ~80% of a campaign's
result**; copy only delivers it. Goal: a "Grand Slam Offer" — so good the reader feels stupid
saying no, and every number in it is true.

## 1. Value equation
Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood) ÷ (Time delay × Effort & sacrifice)

| Lever | For a Kern County office / clinic / warehouse manager | How R&S moves it |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome ↑ | Walk in every morning to a building that's already clean; never chase a janitor; no tenant/patient/boss complaints; pass inspections | Say the outcome, not the chores ("so that" ladder) |
| Likelihood ↑ | "Will this new vendor actually show up and do it right?" | Free work first (they see it), owner does the walkthrough, written room-by-room plan, 24-hour re-clean guarantee, no long contract |
| Time delay ↓ | "How long until it's fixed?" | Walkthrough this week; first clean within days |
| Effort ↓ | Getting quotes, meetings, switching vendors | One-word reply; we write the scope; fixed monthly price; no call needed |

## 2. Build the stack (problems → solutions → bundle)
1. **List every obstacle** the buyer hits before, during and after hiring a cleaner (bad past
   cleaners, hidden fees, long contracts, missed areas, switching hassle, not knowing the price,
   no time to manage it, fear of being locked in).
2. **Turn each into a solution** ("you never pay for a missed area — we come back in 24h free").
3. **Trim & stack:** keep the solutions that are high value to them and low cost to R&S; bundle
   them under one name.

## 3. Enhancers (only if TRUE)
- **Scarcity:** "We only take on N new buildings a month so the owner can walk each one" — only if
  that is the real capacity limit.
- **Urgency:** a real date ("free deep clean for buildings booked by Oct 31").
- **Guarantees:** unconditional (money back), conditional (re-clean within 24h), anti-guarantee
  (no refunds on deep clean but it's free anyway), implied (pay only if…). R&S currently offers the
  conditional 24-hour re-clean. Never promise one R&S can't afford to honor.
- **Bonuses:** each with its own name and a real dollar value (what it would cost them to buy).

## 4. Naming (M-A-G-I-C)
**M**agnetic reason why · **A**vatar · **G**oal · **I**nterval · **C**ontainer word.
E.g. "Kern County Clean Start — free deep clean + 30-day trial for local offices".

## 5. Dollar values — the honesty rule
- A stated value must equal what R&S would actually charge for that piece (price list or real past
  quotes) for the building size named. "Worth $10,000" may only appear if the stacked pieces truly
  add to that for that size of building — otherwise it is a deceptive claim (FTC) and kills trust.
- Prefer "worth $X for a ~N sq ft office" over a single headline number.
- R&S's cost to deliver the free pieces must be known and capped (crew hours), and the owner must
  have capacity for the walkthroughs/deep cleans the campaign creates.

## 6. Putting it in a cold email
The email does NOT list the whole stack. Lead with the single strongest give-first piece (free deep
clean), add one risk-reversal line (24h guarantee / no contract), and one soft CTA. The rest of the
stack is revealed on the walkthrough and in the written plan.
