built from the owner's sources:

**Known errors in the manual — do not repeat them:**
- Its Section 6 critiqued an email R&S never sent ("My name is Ruben Ochoa… 15-minute call"). The
  real control email is in `docs/campaign-learnings.md`.
- Several of its R&S drafts promise things R&S does not do (digital checklists after every shift,
  "medical-grade disinfectant standards", a "1-page sanitization protocol PDF", "unlocked back doors")
  or fake observations ("noticed you expanded on Truxtun Ave"). Use only claims the owner confirms.
- Its stats (e.g. "80% of sales happen on touch 5–12") are source talking points, not verified data.

## Rules (by area)
**List & deliverability**
- Segment before writing (office / medical / industrial / property managers; by size and role).
- Harder-to-get lists (state/county registers, Google Maps, licensing data) reply more than
  "ran-through" database leads.
- Verify every email before sending (e.g. MillionVerifier). Bounce > 2–3% hurts reputation;
  > 10% risks blacklisting. Stop and scrub at > 3%.
- Never send from the main domain; avoid .info; 14-day warm-up; cap Google inboxes ~15–25/day.

**Subject line** — short, lowercase, peer-to-peer, matches the body: `quick question`,
`janitorial question`, `quick question about {{companyName}}'s office`, `free deep clean for {{companyName}}`.
Never title-case marketing lines ("Commercial Cleaning Services For Your Building").

**Opening line** — *Them first* (their company/situation before ours) and *Only them* (if it could go
to 1,000 businesses unchanged, rewrite it). No "My name is…", no fake personalization.

**Offer** — sell the state change, not the chores ("so that" ladder: room-by-room plan → nothing
missed → no complaints / passed inspections). Give first: free walkthrough, free deep clean, sample
scope. Pain ("the stick") beats gain ("the carrot") with cold strangers.

**Proof** — specific and honest: local owner, owner walks every site, written fixed price, 24h
guarantee. Never invent stats or clients.

**CTA** — one soft, permission-based ask answerable in 3 seconds: "Mind if I send it over?",
"Would it make sense to…?", "Reply yes and tell me what day works." No calendar booking ask and
ideally no links in the first email.

**Format** — 50–80 words (up to ~120 for a regional offer), 1–2 sentence paragraphs, plain text,
passes the "drunk test" (gets it in 10 seconds while distracted).

**Follow-ups (sources)** — 3–5 touches 2–3 days apart, each with a NEW angle or free resource; never
"just checking in". (R&S currently runs one email per cycle by owner decision — see SKILL.md.)

**Replies** — answer within 5 minutes; call if there's a phone number; use ACA (Acknowledge → Context → Ask with two specific times + calendar link as backup).

## Frameworks (templates)
- **Instantly 5-line:** observation/trigger → common problem for their role (2 lines) → low-friction offer → soft CTA.
- **Männik 3-line:** pain → risk-free solution/outcome → soft yes/no CTA.
- **Velocityflow 5-line:** relevance hook/pain question → result/state change → proof or mechanism → risk reversal + soft CTA.
- **Segment audit (before writing):** #1 pain · top priority (speed/cost/compliance) · their words · proof/offer that lands.

## Examples from the sources (verbatim)
1. *quick question* — "Hi {{firstName}}, Most founders I work with at that 10 to 30 person range hit that same wall. Outbound worked great when the founder did it personally, but it completely breaks when you try to hand it off. I built a playbook specifically for this transition, and it's the same thing that we helped a similar company do to go from five replies a week to 30 a day. Could I shoot it over?"
2. *product line question* — "Hi {{firstName}}, Saw you just launched the new product line. Congrats. Most brands I work with see a 20 to 30% drop in reply rates when they scale to multiple products. It's usually a segmentation issue. Would it make sense to send over the three-step audit that we use to fix this? It takes 10 minutes just to implement on your end."
3. *saw TikTok doing this what are your thoughts* — "Hey Mark, I saw TikTok is scaling fast, I bet hiring SDRs for Facebook is probably a pain. Most teams lose 70% in 6 months, we found a way to cut that to 20%. Can I send you the breakdown? Reply with yes and it is flying to your inbox."
4. *Andrew quick question about your lead flow* — "Hi Andrew, Can I send you the exact script that got a similar company 82 meetings last month? If I can book you 10 qualified sales calls this month you keep them, if not you do not pay. Mind if I send over an example?"
5. **Commercial cleaning (Leilua)** *Free deep clean for {{companyName}}* — "Hi {{firstName}}, We are offering a deep clean at no cost for {{companyName}} before any recurring services ever start, just to show you the quality of our work. All you have to do is tell us what time to show up. Would you be open to having us come by this week?"
6. *medspa valuation question* — "Hi {{firstName}}, A lot of practice owners are getting calls from buyers right now, and most have no idea what their actual valuation number is. We put together a no-cost evaluation showing exactly what your practice would be worth in the current market. Do you mind if I send it over?"
7. *sample parts for {{companyName}}* — "Hi {{firstName}}, Instead of telling you our parts are better quality, we'd rather just send you five sample products to test before you even consider switching suppliers. Should I drop these off at your facility this week?"
8. *quick 2-min video for {{firstName}}* — "Hi {{firstName}}, I put together a quick 2-minute video showing the exact strategy we used to help {{caseStudyClient}} achieve {{specificMetricResult}}. Mind if I send it over?"
9. Follow-up, new data angle (*Re: quick question*) — "One more thing—just ran the numbers on a similar company's campaign. The one thing that drove 90% of the results was segmenting by region in the first line… Happy to share the breakdown for you if interested."
10. Break-up / free resource (*Re: quick question*) — "Last one for me—the timing's off, I totally get it. But figured I'd send this over regardless… No call required. If you end up using it and want to chat down the road, I'm around."

Patterns that make them work: under ~60 words, lowercase subject, no self-intro, one specific pain or
observation, a give-first asset or sample, risk reversal, a yes/no CTA.

## Reply scripts (R&S, ACA)
- **Yes:** "Awesome. I can come by for a quick walkthrough Wednesday at 10am or Thursday at 2pm and put together your room-by-room plan. Does either work? If easier: {{calendarLink}}"
- **Send pricing:** "Fair question. Our price is a fixed monthly number based on your layout and how often you want service, so I'd need a quick walkthrough to give you an exact figure — and you keep the written plan either way. Wednesday 10am or Thursday 2pm?"
- **Already have a cleaner:** "Makes sense — most well-run buildings do. Plenty of places keep us as a backup or second opinion for when their crew misses. Want me to send a sample room-by-room plan so you have a local benchmark on file?"
- **Not interested:** "Understood — thanks for letting me know. I'll take {{companyName}} off my list. If you ever need a local backup cleaner, I'm here." (and suppress)
- **Wrong person:** "Thanks for the heads up! Who handles cleaning or facilities at {{companyName}}?"

## Testing
Order: subject → opener → front-end offer → CTA. 80% control / 20% test. Judge on reply rate and
positive reply rate; sources ask ~500–1,000 sends per variant (R&S minimum: 300 delivered, ≥3 days
old). Targets: bounce < 2%, reply > 3% (good 8%+), positive reply > 1.5%.

## Pre-send checklist
- Sent from a secondary domain, verified list, bounce running < 3%
- Lowercase, short subject that matches the body
- Opener is about them and passes "only them"
- One give-first offer, outcome-framed, every claim true
- One soft CTA, no meeting ask; no link in email 1 (or a single plain link at most)
- ≤ 80–120 words, 1–2 sentence paragraphs, plain text
- Footer address + "reply no thanks" opt-out; signed Ruben Ochoa
- Owner ready to answer a YES within 5 minutes
